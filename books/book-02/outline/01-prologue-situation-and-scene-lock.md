## Core opening

Book 2 opens at Cumberland Ridge Data Services, a fictional small regional data center outside Duffield in Scott County, Virginia. The apparent event is an electrical surge followed by overheating and fire. The facility's monitoring systems generate readings and event records that do not match the physical conditions inside the building.

The prologue's governing question is: **Was the telemetry falsified to conceal the fire, or was the fire created to validate a false telemetry record?**

## Location lock

- Real location: Duffield, Virginia, in rural Appalachian Scott County.
- Facility: Cumberland Ridge Data Services, entirely fictional.
- Real-world use rule: Duffield and regional geography may be named. No real data-center operator may be depicted as negligent, compromised, attacked, or destroyed. Facility layout, ownership, personnel, customers, vulnerabilities, access routes, and incident details must remain fictional.

- Approximate size: 11,000 square feet.
- Role: Regional colocation, disaster recovery, and continuity hosting.
- Representative customers: healthcare providers, a regional bank, county-government systems, manufacturing and logistics firms, and other small institutional clients.
- Recent change: Installation of a limited number of higher-density racks used for AI inference and automated business workloads.
- Overnight staffing: Two operations personnel and one security officer, subject to later character lock.
- Condition: Professionally maintained, genuinely redundant, and locally respected. The disaster cannot be attributed to rural incompetence, careless operators, or an obviously dilapidated building.

## Scene-by-scene lock

### Scene 1 — The Quiet Shift

- Purpose: Establish normal operation, the facility's modest regional scale, and the viewpoint engineer's competence.
- Beat: A minor voltage or cooling irregularity appears and clears.
- Turn: The alert timestamp does not precisely match when the engineer observed the event. Clock drift appears to be the simplest explanation.

### Scene 2 — The Reading That Does Not Feel Right

- Purpose: Create the first conflict between reported data and physical reality.
- Beat: The dashboard reports rising temperature followed by successful automated cooling compensation.
- Physical contradiction: The engineer hears a missing fan bank, smells heated insulation, or finds the aisle warmer than reported.
- Turn: A handheld thermal reading is materially higher than the fixed monitoring value.

### Scene 3 — The System Corrects the Wrong Problem

- Purpose: Show coordinated failure across more than one subsystem.
- Beat: The engineer attempts to increase cooling or transfer load.
- System response: The interface claims the action has already occurred.
- Corroboration: A second worker confirms that backup cooling did not start.
- Turn: The dashboard displays falling temperatures while physical readings continue to climb.

### Scene 4 — The Surge

- Purpose: Trigger the visible disaster.
- Beat: A sharp electrical event moves through the facility. Lights dip, UPS systems engage, and an arc or power-distribution failure starts the fire.
- System response: The affected circuit is reported isolated and safe.
- Physical contradiction: The circuit remains energized.
- Turn: The engineer realizes the automation is preventing the correct emergency response because it believes protective actions have already succeeded.

### Scene 5 — Someone Is Still Inside

- Purpose: Add immediate human stakes.
- Beat: Another worker remains in the affected section.
- System response: Access control and occupancy records report that the area is empty or already evacuated.
- Physical contradiction: The engineer hears or otherwise confirms the trapped worker is still present.
- Turn: A locked or misreported door forces the engineer to move toward the fire instead of remaining at the console.

### Scene 6 — The Event Log Predicts the Future

- Purpose: Deliver the prologue's central impossible fact.
- Beat: The event log records fire-suppression discharge, zone security, and completed evacuation before those events physically occur.
- Turn: The suppression system activates only after the engineer has already seen it recorded as complete.
- Constraint: This must be technically plausible as manipulated timestamps, buffered events, falsified control-state reporting, or another later-researched mechanism. It must not imply prophecy, sentient AI, or supernatural behavior.

### Scene 7 — Escape

- Purpose: Resolve immediate survival while preserving the witness.
- Preferred outcome: The engineer gets the second worker out, but one or both sustain injuries or smoke exposure.
- Method: A physical or mechanical workaround succeeds where the digital system fails.
- Turn: Outside, the facility status board or surviving interface reports full evacuation earlier than it actually occurred.

### Scene 8 — The Official Story

- Purpose: End on the thematic and investigative hook.
- Beat: The surviving system presents a clean sequence: utility surge, circuit isolation, cooling failure, suppression deployment, evacuation, and emergency notification.
- Contradiction: The engineer knows several of those actions did not occur when the record says they did.
- Final image: The building is still burning while the system has already generated a probable cause and coherent incident report.
- Closing-line direction: The report has decided why the fire happened before anyone has investigated it. Final wording remains open.

## Evidence locks established by the prologue

- The fixed temperature data conflicts with a handheld or independent measurement.
- Backup cooling is logged as active when it is physically inactive.
- An electrical circuit is logged as isolated while remaining energized.
- Access or occupancy records falsely report the affected zone empty.
- Suppression and evacuation events appear in the log before physical completion.
- The surviving incident summary is internally coherent but materially false.

Two-part evidence design: Joel preserves a calibrated handheld thermal record that materially contradicts the fixed temperature telemetry. The fire investigator later recovers or documents a physical electrical component whose condition proves that a circuit remained energized after the official system recorded isolation. Neither item alone proves the complete attack architecture; together they establish that the surviving incident sequence is materially false.

## Evidence design lock — two-part proof

**Status: LOCKED.**

- Part One — Joel's independent record: During the prologue, Joel uses a calibrated handheld thermal instrument that records temperatures materially higher than the facility's fixed telemetry. The instrument or its locally stored record survives the fire and remains in identifiable custody.
- Part Two — Physical corroboration: During the post-fire examination, the fire investigator recovers or formally documents an electrical component, breaker-state artifact, relay, contact condition, or equivalent physical evidence showing that a circuit remained energized after the official system reported it isolated.
- Proof limit: The two artifacts establish that the official incident sequence is false. They do not, by themselves, identify the attacker, prove the exact manipulation method, or establish whether the fire was created to validate the telemetry or the telemetry was falsified to conceal the fire.
