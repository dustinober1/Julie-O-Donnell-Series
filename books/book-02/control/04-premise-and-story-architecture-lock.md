COMMERCIAL STORY FOUNDATION

Core Premise

Julie O'Donnell accepts her first major independent mandate through a joint United States–Canadian review commission after an emergency model predicts cascading dam failures across a fictional Pacific Northwest river system shared by both countries.

The forecast triggers a massive evacuation, emergency control of the river corridor, restricted airspace, cross-border military and civilian response, and disruption of regional power operations. During the cleared window, the scientist who designed the regional disaster-modeling platform disappears from a secure Canadian monitoring facility.

Julie is officially retained to determine whether the cascade-failure warning was supported by authentic source data, identify where the evidence chain first became unreliable, and preserve findings suitable for proceedings in both countries. Her written mandate expressly excludes investigation of the scientist's disappearance.

Julie eventually proves that the warning was deliberately engineered to create the empty corridor needed to extract the scientist. She then faces a harder problem: a real hostile cyber-physical compromise activates inside the same dam system, forcing her to persuade two governments to act on a genuine warning delivered through evidence she has just proved unreliable.

Commercial Hook

A source-integrity investigator must expose a manufactured dam catastrophe without destroying trust in the warning system when the dam begins to fail for real—and when she discovers the supposedly evacuated valley is not empty.

Standalone and Series Function

Book 2 must close the central dam case sufficiently to satisfy as a complete novel. It advances the larger series conspiracy through doctrine, consequences, and bounded evidence rather than solving the original 02:14 construction, Sterling's personal command, Vance's upstream role, or every Book 1 unresolved thread.

The dam investigation remains the governing plot. The western Pacific confrontation supplies strategic consequence and the ending transition into Book 3, but it must not displace Julie's investigation, the scientist's extraction, the river corridor, or the dam climax.

CENTRAL THEMATIC AND EVIDENTIARY PRINCIPLE

The Book 2 manipulation does not primarily invent sensor readings. It selects authentic readings, excludes conflicting observations, changes admission thresholds, alters weighting, aggregates categories, and shapes which facts are allowed to enter the official model.

Individual visible records may be authentic. The fraud exists in the relationship among them and in the decision architecture that converts real evidence into a predetermined conclusion.

The method echoes Veridrift at the level of doctrine:

• trusted systems can produce a false reality without every input being false;

• source selection and reconciliation can be more dangerous than obvious fabrication;

• institutional authority can convert uncertain interpretation into operational fact;

• a truthful warning can become unusable after evidence systems are deliberately corrupted.

This resemblance does not, by itself, prove shared code, a common operator, the original 02:14 constructor, Senator Sterling's personal involvement, Arthur Vance's upstream role, or a single organization controlling both books.

All systems remain non-sentient. Human design, access, incentives, governance, and operational decisions create the threat.

SETTING AND GEOPOLITICAL FRAME

Primary Setting

The primary setting is a fictional Pacific Northwest river and hydroelectric system spanning the United States–Canada border. The setting should include:

• interconnected dams and reservoirs;

• regional power markets and transmission dependencies;

• mountain communities and constrained road access;

• cross-border emergency protocols;

• Indigenous territories and governments with independent rights and authority;

• federal, provincial, state, local, military, utility, and law-enforcement jurisdictions;

• secure monitoring and emergency-command facilities;

• severe weather and difficult terrain;

• an emergency spillway corridor capable of producing a controlled but destructive flood.

All river, dam, community, agency-program, and commission names remain TBD and must be fictionalized after technical and jurisdictional research.

Secondary Strategic Theater

A fictional democratic island state in the western Pacific produces ultra-advanced semiconductors critical to civilian electronics, communications, aerospace, infrastructure repair, and defense systems. Its security depends on an ambiguous network of allied commitments rather than a simple guaranteed-defense formula.

A hostile power stages a major military exercise around the island. The exercise hardens into a gray-zone blockade through inspection zones, air and maritime restrictions, cyber disruption, shipping pressure, and claims of temporary safety enforcement. The objective is to isolate the island without presenting allied governments with one clean moment that unmistakably begins a war.

The hostile North American infrastructure campaign is intended to delay recognition, communications, port and fuel operations, military movement, and political unity during the blockade's decisive opening hours.

Book 2 should show the blockade mainly through intelligence fragments, limited cutaways, market disruption, military alerts, and the final conversion of exercise into blockade. Julie does not investigate the island crisis directly in this book.

THE MISSING SCIENTIST

Function

The missing scientist designed the legitimate national or binational platform that fuses:

• dam instrumentation;

• reservoir and hydrological data;

• weather forecasts;

• satellite observations;

• structural and mechanical readings;

• power-generation conditions;

• evacuation models;

• route and population data;

• emergency-response assumptions.

She discovers that a concealed decision layer has been inserted into the live system. The layer can turn legitimate uncertainty into a convincing catastrophe forecast by controlling which authentic inputs are admitted and how they are weighted.

Why She Is Extracted Alive

The concealed layer is an incomplete, infrastructure-specific prototype. Her captors need her to make the method portable so it can manipulate other crisis systems, including wildfire prediction, flood forecasting, grid instability, chemical-release modeling, evacuation routing, and military disaster response.

She did not create the rogue operational layer. She understands the legitimate platform deeply enough to stabilize, generalize, or defeat it.

Pre-Extraction Conduct

Believing official reporting channels were compromised, she copied restricted records without authorization and assembled an air-gapped proof package containing raw sensor captures, archived weather and satellite inputs, model-version histories, administrator-access records, authentication information, and her reconstruction of the concealed layer.

Her conduct gives investigators a legitimate reason to consider whether she was a whistleblower, security risk, thief, or prospective defector. The Canadian co-lead had already begun examining her conduct before the evacuation.

The captors remove the physical proof package with her. A manifest and cryptographic checksum record survive. Those records establish that a defined archive existed and was deliberately removed, but they do not independently prove every claimed file's contents.

Scientist POV and Agency

The scientist must not disappear for most of the novel and return as a passive rescue objective. She should receive approximately five to seven controlled POV chapters totaling roughly 10,000–14,000 words.

Her captivity story should show her:

• identifying what the rogue faction needs from her;

• pretending to cooperate;

• inserting faults or limits into the portable architecture;

• preserving fragments or traces of her proof;

• learning that the external hostile threat is real;

• creating the anomaly Julie eventually follows;

• attempting resistance or escape that fails but changes the operational situation;

• contributing materially to the final separation of authentic sabotage from manipulated model output.

Identity Rule

The scientist's name remains TBD. The name “Mara Venn” was explicitly rejected and must not be used. Her name should be selected only after nationality, age, family background, education, career history, professional reputation, personality, and cultural context are established, followed by an internal and external name-collision screen.

JULIE'S LAWFUL MANDATE

Sponsoring Authority

Julie is retained by a newly formed joint United States–Canadian investigative or review commission. “Joint Cascade Review Commission” may be used as a temporary descriptive placeholder but is not a locked final name.

The commission exists because neither government fully trusts the other's preliminary evidence, and both require a technically independent record capable of supporting their separate legal and administrative processes.

Mandate Scope

Julie's written mandate authorizes her to:

• determine whether the cascade-failure warning was supported by authentic source data;

• identify the earliest point at which the evidence chain became unreliable;

• preserve source ownership, objections, withheld categories, and proof ceilings;

• produce findings suitable for separate U.S. and Canadian proceedings;

• recommend a scope change through a named lawful authority when new evidence requires it.

The mandate excludes the scientist's disappearance. That exclusion is deliberate and later becomes evidence that the post-event inquiry was anticipated.

Book 1 Compatibility

Book 2 opens approximately eight months after October 16, in late June. The exact calendar date and opening clock remain TBD. Julie's wrist injury, unresolved federal review, clearance restrictions, young independent practice, and returning-character consent boundaries remain active consequences rather than resolved history.

Julie begins without a standing clearance, universal credential, permanent government role, immunity, authority over her own unresolved case, or automatic access to either nation's systems.

Her terms of work remain binding:

• every case begins with a written mandate;

• source originals remain with lawful custodians;

• proof limits, clock bases, objections, and withheld categories travel with findings;

• participants retain counsel, consent, custody of personal acts, and an enforceable right to stop;

• prior usefulness creates no future claim;

• Julie performs no work on her own unresolved Book 1 case.

THE CANADIAN CO-LEAD

Julie is paired with a senior Royal Canadian Mounted Police major-crimes investigator who controls lawful access to Canadian facilities, witnesses, vehicles, surveillance, emergency records, and physical evidence.

The investigator is not Julie's Canadian equivalent and is not subordinate to her.

Investigative Contrast

• Julie reconstructs what systems claimed happened.

• The RCMP investigator reconstructs what people physically did.

• Julie distrusts evidence that reconciles too neatly.

• The investigator distrusts theories that cannot yet be attached to a person, vehicle, place, witness, or criminal act.

• Each initially believes the other is overlooking the real case.

• Their methods eventually become mutually necessary.

Prior Connection to the Scientist

Before the evacuation, the investigator was examining the scientist's unauthorized copying of restricted infrastructure records and outside contacts. The investigator had not determined whether she was preparing a protected disclosure, committing espionage, or planning to defect.

This gives the investigator a legitimate reason to resist Julie's early abduction theory.

The investigator's name, rank, service history, family state, and personal pressure remain TBD.

TEAM STRUCTURE AND CONSENT

Marcus Reed

Marcus may join Julie's field work only through a credible medical, legal, administrative, and relational path from his Book 1 endpoint. His return does not automatically restore command, forgiveness, romance, or the prior partnership.

His role is physical verification, field observation, witness development, route reconstruction, off-system checks, and protection within the actual mandate.

Elias Thorne

Elias may participate only after his counsel communicates consent and the case terms are explicit. He begins as a restricted outside technical consultant, not a standing team member. His Apex history makes both governments reluctant to grant him direct system access.

Julie initially gives him narrow, isolated technical questions without disclosing the full parallel-investigation theory. This compartmentation is operationally defensible and personally damaging.

Parallel Inquiry Team

After the midpoint discovery that Julie's commission mandate was prewritten, she tells Marcus and the RCMP co-lead enough to establish a concealed parallel inquiry. Marcus handles physical verification; the Canadian investigator uses lawful authority; Julie controls proof thresholds and separation from the official record.

Elias remains compartmented longer because his communications and technical work are monitored and because the commission may be compromised.

Julie never falsifies an official finding, feeds false evidence, or creates a universal evidence file. She continues producing technically accurate official work while independently pursuing lawful sources outside the commission's curated evidence boundary.

ANTAGONIST ARCHITECTURE

Legitimate Program

A deniable Western intelligence program was created to red-team allied critical-infrastructure and disaster-warning systems. Its legitimate mission was to determine whether adversaries could manipulate emergency systems without fabricating obvious sensor data.

Most personnel associated with the program believe they are conducting defensive research and vulnerability testing.

Rogue Compartment

A small compartment turns the defensive capability into an operational weapon. Its doctrine is to manufacture credible emergencies that cause governments to move themselves:

• evacuate a location;

• redirect police, military, aviation, and emergency resources;

• close roads and airspace;

• suspend normal oversight;

• isolate a target;

• create classified aftermaths before anyone understands the original event.

The faction believes a major coordinated attack on North American infrastructure is approaching and that normal democratic authorization will come too late.

Operational Commander

The primary human antagonist is the respected operational commander of the rogue compartment. The commander correctly identified the external infrastructure threat and concluded that lawful government could not respond quickly enough.

The commander authorizes:

• insertion or use of the concealed decision layer;

• the manufactured cascade warning;

• extraction and coercion of the scientist;

• use of emergency systems and the mutual-aid registry;

• shaping the evacuation boundary to preserve the extraction route;

• Julie's selection as an unwitting independent validator.

The commander should appear early under an official liaison, sponsor, or emergency-program role. They support Julie's appointment, defend her access, provide genuinely useful intelligence, and appear to be one of the few officials willing to let her follow the evidence.

The commander expected Julie to prove the warning was manipulated, discover authentic hostile compromises, attribute the entire crisis to the hostile service, and provide credible grounds for allied mobilization. They underestimated her ability to separate the rogue faction's earlier operation from the later hostile activation.

Antagonist Logic

The commander is right that the external danger is real. That truth does not authorize kidnapping, coercion, corrupted evidence, manufactured evacuation, or unilateral control over civilians and public institutions.

The commander's argument is:

They were right about the attack. Therefore, they believe they were right about what they did to prepare for it.

End State

The commander escapes during the dam crisis through emergency infrastructure prepared for the extraction.

The escape costs the commander:

• the scientist;

• the full portable system;

• the exposed dam operation;

• arrested, killed, or abandoned personnel;

• the commander's official identity and position;

• Julie's ignorance of the doctrine.

The commander retains a damaged partial copy of the portable decision-layer architecture. It can support smaller targeted manipulations when paired with local access, preparation, authentic data, and infrastructure expertise. It cannot independently create another continent-scale crisis.

THE REAL EXTERNAL THREAT

A hostile intelligence service has spent years embedding dormant, site-specific cyber-physical access inside North American infrastructure, including:

• dams;

• substations and grid-control systems;

• ports and logistics systems;

• satellite ground stations;

• emergency communications;

• regional command and control centers.

There is no single master switch. The campaign consists of distributed compromises that appear individually as equipment failures, operator error, isolated cyber incidents, or routine software defects.

Activated together, they can blind and immobilize North America during the opening of the western Pacific blockade.

The hostile threat is real. The rogue faction's response is unlawful.

Causal Activation Rule

The hostile compromise inside the dam must not activate early by coincidence.

The rogue faction's unauthorized intrusion, attempt to isolate the dam network, change in control conditions, or access to a protected layer triggers a dormant hostile contingency or alerts the hostile service, causing the dam attack to begin ahead of the wider schedule.

The collision between the manufactured crisis and the real failure must therefore be a consequence of human choices made during the rogue operation.

INVESTIGATION AND EVIDENCE ARCHITECTURE

Midpoint Reveal

Near the midpoint, Julie discovers metadata proving that core language in the commission's founding package and her appointment existed before the false warning.

The prewritten material anticipates:

• the precise source-integrity question;

• the expected U.S.–Canadian dispute;

• access to selected model repositories;

• exclusion of the scientist's disappearance;

• Julie as the preferred independent investigator.

This does not by itself prove who caused the crisis. It proves that someone anticipated the investigation's exact shape and intended to control its boundaries.

Julie's Response

Julie remains inside the commission and runs two cases at once:

• the official inquiry into whether the warning was supported by authentic evidence;

• the concealed inquiry into who designed the commission's evidentiary boundaries before the emergency.

She does not confront leadership prematurely, resign, or issue a knowingly false conclusion.

Primary Physical Clue

Marcus discovers evidence that an emergency command vehicle entered the restricted corridor before the evacuation order.

Its Canadian emergency identity is genuine, but the physical vehicle belongs to a classified American fleet. The rogue faction inserted it months earlier into a legitimate cross-border mutual-aid registry. During a declared disaster, its credentials automatically provide access to restricted roads, fuel and maintenance sites, border exemptions, protected communications, and emergency facilities.

The record is authentic. The relationship between the record and the vehicle is false.

Proof Package and Manifest

The scientist's proof package and its surviving manifest must have separate proof ceilings.

The manifest can establish:

• that a defined physical archive existed;

• that specific file names, source claims, timestamps, and checksums were recorded;

• that the archive was removed;

• that the scientist took deliberate preservation steps.

It cannot, without recovered originals or independent reconstruction, establish:

• that every listed file contained what the scientist claimed;

• that every source was authentic;

• who inserted the concealed layer;

• who ordered the extraction;

• common authorship with Veridrift.

Omitted Settlement Mechanics

The rogue faction must not simply fabricate evacuation delivery receipts if the same result can be achieved through selection, aggregation, and categorization.

The stronger mechanism is:

• the original evacuation polygon is shaped so a small settlement remains outside the predicted spillway corridor;

• models and authentic readings supporting that boundary receive greater weight;

• the settlement's only viable road remains open for the scientist's extraction convoy;

• broader regional dashboards later label the sector “cleared” using authentic aggregate traffic, utility, and emergency-status data rather than direct confirmation from every resident;

• when real hostile sabotage expands the required spillway release, the settlement falls inside the true flood path;

• Julie identifies the missing direct acknowledgments and the category boundary before the gates open.

The official dashboard can be internally consistent and still materially wrong.

Julie, Marcus, and the RCMP investigator reach the site while the rogue faction is attempting to sanitize it. Severe weather, damaged infrastructure, or an emergency road closure traps or isolates them.

CLIMAX LOCK

Location

The climax occurs inside and around an operating hydroelectric dam. The scientist is held in restricted galleries beneath the powerhouse or another technically credible secured level connected to live control systems.

Real Failure

The rogue faction believes it controls the manufactured dam emergency. Its interference triggers the dormant hostile compromise, which changes gate positions, turbine loads, reservoir conditions, pressure, or other researched mechanical variables beyond the faction's model.

The dam is genuinely failing.

Julie cannot save the situation merely by announcing that the original warning was false. She must determine what is real, what remains uncertain, and which action can still prevent structural collapse.

Emergency Action

The only viable response is to open an emergency spillway and deliberately send a controlled flood through the evacuated valley.

The release will destroy homes, roads, substations, and other infrastructure, but refusing it risks uncontrolled dam failure and far greater casualties.

Julie must identify the trustworthy evacuation boundary and the final irreversible threshold. The actual release decision must remain with the lawful dam or emergency authority unless research establishes a different credible mechanism. Julie recommends and proves; she does not acquire unsupported command authority.

Settlement Rescue

One settlement remains occupied because the rogue faction shaped the original evacuation corridor to preserve the scientist's extraction route.

Marcus and the RCMP investigator race to clear it while Julie determines how long the dam can survive. The gates may need to open while rescuers are still close to or inside the flood corridor.

Scientist's Role

After rescue, the scientist materially assists in distinguishing genuine sabotage signals from the manipulated model and in identifying the limits she inserted into the portable architecture.

Outcome

• the settlement is cleared;

• the controlled flood is released;

• the main dam survives;

• the scientist survives;

• the full North American cascade is prevented;

• significant property and infrastructure are lost;

• the commander escapes;

• the western Pacific blockade begins.

JULIE'S PERSONAL ARC

Primary Arc

Julie must learn that controlled secrecy can be necessary and still damage trust.

After discovering that the commission is compromised, she treats information control as a form of protecting her team. Her reasons are defensible. Her treatment of the people accepting risk is not fully defensible.

The relationship damage must differ:

Marcus

Marcus accepts physical danger without understanding enough of the strategic stakes. His issue is informed consent. He tells Julie that she does not get to decide what risk he is entitled to understand.

RCMP Co-Lead

The Canadian investigator uses lawful authority based on Julie's requests while being denied facts that could have changed professional judgment. The issue is institutional and legal trust.

Elias

Elias is given isolated technical problems without the purpose or context of his work. He feels reduced to an instrument—the same fundamental injury Apex inflicted when it valued what he could authenticate, access, and repair more than his agency.

Team Fracture

All three relationships fracture before the climax. The immediate danger requires them to continue working together, but crisis cooperation does not erase the harm.

Ending Relationship State

• Marcus remains, but future work requires enough disclosure for meaningful consent.

• The RCMP investigator may work with Julie again, but no longer grants automatic trust.

• Elias does not reconcile fully and leaves Julie's practice.

• Julie apologizes without demanding forgiveness or claiming that a good outcome justified her choices.

ELIAS'S END STATE

Elias joins the rescued scientist to establish an independent critical-infrastructure audit laboratory.

The laboratory is designed to:

• rebuild the lost proof package through lawful reconstruction;

• audit high-risk disaster and infrastructure systems over time;

• maintain explicit context, consent, custody, and purpose for technical work;

• preserve source ownership and proof limits;

• contribute to defense against the real hostile campaign;

• operate outside Julie's authority.

This is not an antagonist turn or a punishment. Elias chooses a structure that addresses the exact boundary Julie violated.

He remains relevant to the series and may share objectives with Julie, but he is not automatically available for Book 3.

TECHNOLOGY AND REALISM LIMITS

Portable Decision Layer

The portable capability requires:

• privileged or local access;

• authentic input streams;

• infrastructure-specific preparation;

• technical knowledge of the legitimate platform;

• a defined operational objective;

• time to shape admission, weighting, aggregation, and response assumptions.

It cannot generate any emergency anywhere on command. The commander's escaped partial copy supports smaller, targeted manipulations only when paired with insiders, access, and preparation.

Hostile Campaign

The hostile compromise is distributed and site-specific. Some sites may share tools or doctrine, but the story must not reduce the campaign to one universal exploit or master switch.

Evidence

Every major technical finding must state:

• source;

• custodian;

• clock basis;

• authenticity basis;

• what the evidence proves;

• what it does not prove;

• who has seen it;

• derivative status;

• objections and withheld categories;

• alternative explanations still open.

Research Requirement

No detailed dam operation, failure mechanism, spillway action, cyber-physical exploit, RCMP procedure, border exemption, or military response should be locked solely from intuition. Research and specialist review must precede chapter-level technical commitments.

## Permissible Force, Weapons, Tactical Response, Medical Duty, and Disengagement During the RCMP Climax Operation: Integrated Matrix with Independent Deadly-Force Threshold

Controlling architecture

The RCMP climax operation uses a continuously reassessed force-and-disengagement matrix rather than a single escalating continuum. Every action must satisfy four requirements: a currently lawful Canadian objective; a current factual basis; necessity after reasonable alternatives are considered; and proportionality to the immediate conduct and danger.

Authorization under Decision 136 permits the intervention to begin. It does not predetermine how much force may be used. Decision 137 remains continuously operative: force must pause, narrow, or end when authentication, legal authority, safety, or the active trigger no longer supports it.

Category 1 — Presence, positioning, barriers, and communication

Category 2 — Non-force physical intervention

Officers may physically reposition themselves, block a route, shield another person, remove an unattended obstruction, or establish control of an area without intentionally applying force to a person. This category does not authorize touching, restraining, striking, or incapacitating a person.

Category 3 — Proportionate non-deadly force

Non-deadly force may be used only when reasonably necessary for an individualized, lawful Canadian objective and when lesser practical measures are insufficient.

Potential objectives include protecting a person from injury; completing a lawful arrest or detention; controlling active physical resistance; separating a person from the package; stopping a currently authenticated Decision 136 action; preventing escape from established lawful custody; or preventing immediate evidence destruction when the selected method does not create a greater life or package hazard.

Force is directed only at the person whose conduct supports it. One custodian’s resistance does not authorize force against the group.

Passive presence, silence, a request for counsel, refusal to answer, confusion, fear, medical impairment, technical disagreement, or proximity to the package is not resistance by itself.

Category 4 — Deadly force

Deadly force has an independent threshold. It is authorized only when competent Canadian personnel reasonably believe that the targeted person presents an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm and deadly force is necessary to stop that threat.

The following never independently authorize deadly force: commander identity; a Decision 136 trigger; package possession; package movement; evidence destruction; escape; refusal to surrender; refusal to provide credentials; political importance; suspected state affiliation; uncertainty about package operability; or fear that the investigation may fail.

An ambiguous package action cannot be treated as a lethal act merely because the package might be dangerous. The factual basis must support the immediate life-threat conclusion.

Warning and surrender requirement

Before using force, the RCMP gives clear instructions, a surrender opportunity, and a reasonable chance to comply when doing so does not increase the immediate danger or defeat a necessary protective action.

Warnings may be shortened or omitted only when delay would materially increase an imminent threat to life or serious bodily safety. Failure to comply is assessed in context, including whether the person could hear, understand, and physically follow the instructions.

Package-directed tactical actions

Actions directed at the package, its vehicle, connected equipment, or surrounding infrastructure require separate Canadian hazard authorization.

This includes firing at the package or a connected object; forcibly disabling a vehicle carrying it; destructive electronic interference; explosive or destructive entry; deliberate power disruption; actions likely to damage storage media; or actions that could initiate an unknown package response.

An earlier valid justification cannot be stored and reused after conditions change. Any renewed force requires a current factual and legal basis.

Preservation of life overrides capture, questioning, and evidence recovery.

## Missing Component Search, Preservation, and Recovery: Evidence-Led Priority Ladder with Separate Jurisdictional Authority

The joint investigation pursues the missing execution, transmission, or record component through a staged evidence-led priority ladder. The architecture separates preservation, lead development, priority classification, search authorization, recovery and forensic comparison, and status reassessment. The component’s absence creates a focused investigative question; it does not create general authority to search everyone or everything associated with the authenticated commander.

Governing distinctions

Preservation maintains evidence at risk. It does not authorize access, content review, search, seizure, or exploitation.

Association does not establish individualized linkage. Access opportunity does not establish possession. Recovery does not establish authentication. One jurisdiction’s authority does not transfer automatically to another.

Stage 1 — Immediate bounded preservation

Investigators identify records genuinely at risk of routine deletion, overwrite, loss, transfer, or destruction. Specifically bounded preservation may cover surveillance recordings, access-control records, transportation and border records, vehicle records, communications metadata, service-provider connection records, cloud or repository records, shipping and procurement records, employer or facility logs, storage records, and relevant device-management records.

Every preservation request identifies the component-related factual basis, relevant time window, record category, target person, place, device, account, or facility, risk of loss, and governing jurisdiction.

Broad preservation of every record belonging to every associate is prohibited. Preservation does not authorize investigators to read content, access an account, search a device, or seize property.

Stage 2 — Component custody-and-opportunity matrix

The investigation builds a non-conclusive matrix identifying where the component could have been separated, transferred, stored, copied, concealed, or destroyed.

The matrix may use the last authenticated package state showing the component relationship; physical interface residue; validated timestamps; recovery-scene evidence; custody and transport records; camera, access, vehicle, facility, border, and route records; lawful device-pairing evidence; verified communications or meetings; relevant repository events; documented statements; physical dimensions and storage requirements; and transparent technical requirements established through the Decision 138 examination process.

Every entry is classified as authenticated fact, corroborated inference, uncorroborated allegation, unresolved contradiction, or benign alternative explanation.

No person or place enters the matrix solely because of friendship, employment, family connection, political affiliation, shared nationality, shared organization, generic communication, or proximity to the commander.

Stage 3 — Evidence-based priority tiers

Tier 1 — Direct authenticated linkage

A Tier 1 lead contains evidence directly connecting a specific person, place, device, account, repository, route, or transfer event to the missing component. Examples include a component-specific identifier, authenticated pairing evidence, a verified transfer record, video or access evidence showing a compatible object, a package record naming a specific interface or repository, a corroborated statement supported by independent evidence, or sufficiently distinctive physical residue or container evidence.

Tier 1 may support immediate application for the appropriate authority. It does not establish guilt or harmful purpose.

Tier 2 — Corroborated access and opportunity

A Tier 2 lead has at least two independent evidence streams supporting meaningful access and opportunity during the relevant separation period. The evidence may combine physical access, verified presence, technical capability, relevant communication, unusual transfer behavior, and matching transport or storage evidence.

Tier 2 supports focused investigation and may support a warrant only when the applicable legal standard is independently met.

Tier 3 — Contextual investigative lead

A Tier 3 lead is plausible but lacks direct or sufficiently corroborated linkage. It may support voluntary inquiry, lawful public-record review, narrowly bounded preservation when loss risk and authority exist, or comparison against evidence already lawfully held. Tier 3 does not justify an intrusive search merely because the component is important.

Excluded association category

A person or location is excluded from search priority when the connection rests only on family or professional association, shared organization, political disagreement, presence in an address book, generic communications, ordinary travel, nationality, common technical similarity, or proximity without evidence of access.

Stage 4 — Separate authority for every search

Every physical or digital search requires authority tied to a specifically identified target; evidence connecting that target to the missing component; the place, device, account, data, or repository to be searched; the evidence expected to be found; the relevant period; the proposed method; minimization and scope limits; and the governing jurisdiction.

Canadian authorities govern Canadian persons, places, seizures, devices, accounts, and repositories. United States authorities govern American targets through separate American process. Western Pacific partners receive only approved information and must independently establish their authority.

No jurisdiction may rely on another jurisdiction’s warrant, detention, seizure, or investigative label as a substitute for its own lawful basis.

Stage 5 — Independent and narrow emergency authority

Emergency action may occur only when current evidence establishes an independently recognized emergency, such as imminent danger to life, an active destructive process, immediate transfer beyond practical lawful reach, or a technically authenticated risk of irreversible evidence loss.

The component’s importance, political pressure, institutional embarrassment, or uncertainty about a possible attack does not independently create emergency authority.

Emergency action stops when the emergency ends. Continued examination or exploitation requires ordinary lawful authority.

Stage 6 — Recovery and comparison protocol

Any candidate recovered in Canada enters a separate Decision 138-compliant custody and forensic process.

Investigators document the recovery location, condition, and independent custody history; stabilize only as necessary; avoid connecting the candidate directly to the recovered package; obtain authority for examination; acquire and analyze verified working copies where possible; and compare authenticated characteristics through documented methods.

The recovered package and candidate originals are not physically or digitally merged merely to test whether they function together. A successful cryptographic, physical, component, or record relationship is documented as a comparison result without erasing the originals’ separate custody histories.

A foreign recovery remains in that jurisdiction’s custody. Approved derivative findings may be shared, but the Canadian repository does not absorb a foreign original.

Stage 7 — Negative and contradictory results have operational force

An unsuccessful search is not evidence that a person concealed the component elsewhere.

A negative result must reduce or remove the target’s priority when appropriate, enter the joint evidence record, reach decision-makers relying on the earlier lead, trigger review of related detention, arrest, surveillance, or search positions, and prevent the disproven theory from being repeated in another jurisdiction without new evidence.

Contradictions identified by Julie, Elias, examiners, custodians, counsel, or independent reviewers are preserved rather than filtered out of the lead package.

Stage 8 — Commander and custodian status review

Decision 141 does not automatically strengthen or extend every person’s custody status.

The missing-component investigation may affect a person only when evidence specific to that individual supports possession, transfer, concealment, destruction, technical control, a materially relevant false statement, or another legally meaningful act.

The missing component cannot justify indefinite detention while investigators search for evidence. Exculpatory, protective, or benign evidence must be incorporated into the Decision 139 status review.
