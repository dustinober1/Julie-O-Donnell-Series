## 2. A revision to an earlier recommendation

`73-` §2 recommended compressing Chapters 19–21 by 30–40%. **That is withdrawn.** After the Kashmir intercut, Chapter 19 carries `NO INDIAN ROUNDS FIRED` as the payoff for a patrol the reader has now watched climb. Its value rose. Chapter 20 is the tightest chapter in the book and Chapter 21's interior work on Price is characterisation, not procedure.

Only Chapter 19's opening is compressed, and only by 700–900 words.

## 3. Executed changes

1. **Vance embodied in Chapter 20.** A counsel-supervised interview, conducted by Grant, in which he answers nothing. Roughly 1,000 words. **Delivered: +938.**
2. **Chapter 19 opening compressed.** 700–900 words out of the receiving-instrument negotiation, before the acknowledgment opens. **Delivered: −38. The figure was wrong.**
3. **Kashmir scene B trimmed** by roughly 300 words — the field orientation check repeated itself. **Delivered: −74. The figure was wrong.**
4. **No Sterling scene.** Declined; see §5.

> **Correction, on execution.** The two compression figures in items 2 and 3 were not supportable and were not met. Chapter 19 is 2,745 words in total and its opening is 927, so a 700–900-word cut would have deleted the opening outright; Kashmir scene B is 1,149 words whose three movements each do distinct work. Both figures were sized from the rhythm measurement in §1 rather than from a line-level reading of what actually repeats. The redundancy finding stands and the redundancy was cut — 112 words of genuine repetition across the two chapters. The arithmetic did not. See `78-` §3 for the measurements and the itemised cuts.

## 4. Constraints on the Vance scene

Locked. A draft violating any of these is rejected.

1. **Julie is not in the room.** She is restrained, and her counsel undertaking bars her from investigating her own case. The argument of Act III is that she has stopped being the one who carries the record. A Julie–Vance confrontation would read as the payoff and would break the thing the book is about. She may observe through the incident channel and must not intervene.
2. **Vance concedes nothing.** No admission, no slip, no tell that functions as evidence. The record narrows around him without his help. That is the point of putting him in a room.
3. **No new proof, and no pre-emption of Chapter 22.** At this point in the story the release finding does not exist. The interview may reach only what Chapter 16 already established through Sarah's preservation notice: the executive certificate registered to him, the material-loss force addendum, and the occupied-room suppression override. His live authentication of the 07:52 reconstruction stays in Chapter 22, unspoiled.
4. **Proof ceilings hold.** His personal initiation of the 02:14 construction remains unestablished. Sterling remains unestablished. Neither may be narrowed by this scene.
5. **The interview must have a procedural reason to exist.** It happens because a refusal has to be on the record before anyone can characterise him as never having been asked — not because the investigation needs him to talk.
6. **No new named characters.** Grant, Alvarez, Vance and his counsel only.
7. **The ending is untouched.**

## 5. Declined

**A Sterling scene.** The unresolved proof ceiling is the book's spine. Putting him on the page invites precisely the overclaim that Chapter 23's aborted redline exists to refuse, and would convert a deliberate series thread into a dangling one.

## 6. Length effect

Projected: Chapter 19 loses roughly 800 and Chapter 20 gains roughly 1,000, leaving Act III close to flat.

Actual: Chapter 20 gains 938, Chapters 19 and 16 lose 112 between them, and the accepted total rises 826 to **109,498** — inside the 105,000–110,000 target band. Act III is longer than projected because the compression was not available. Its shape still improves: Vance's longest absence falls from five consecutive chapters to three, and the Chapter 17–24 spread narrows to 2,486–3,301.
