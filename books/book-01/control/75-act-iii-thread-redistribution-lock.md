## 1. The measured problem

The review's original framing — "the thriller ends at Chapter 8" — was imprecise. The measurement is sharper and points at a cheaper fix.

Kashmir is the only location in this book where a person can be physically killed. It appears in **Chapters 3, 4, 5 and 8**, then disappears for **42,745 words** (Chapters 9–17), then returns in Chapter 18.

Chapter 8 ends by putting five or six trained people on a mountain moving toward Relay K-17, and by establishing that Julie's own forty-three-second boundary error is part of why they are unobserved. That is a live wire with a human cost attached to the protagonist's mistake. The book then sets it down for nine chapters — exactly the span in which the domestic plot also converts from pursuit to procedure. **Both sources of jeopardy switch off simultaneously.** That is the Act III sag, and it is a thread-management problem, not a genre-contract problem.

A second observation compounds it: when Kashmir returns, the patrol — the ice, Pal's head injury, the observation from Kestrel, the shaved anti-tamper paint at the wicket, the decision not to cross the inner boundary — is delivered as **testimony in a warm room after the fact**. The back half's one sequence of physical jeopardy is reported rather than dramatized.

## 2. Why this is a redistribution and not a rewrite

The chronology already supports the intercut. No timeline changes.

Chapter 18 already sits five minutes *before* Chapter 17 in absolute time. The book presents two concurrent strands sequentially, in blocks, rather than intercut. The patrol's climb occupies the ~28 hours of Kashmir story time between Chapter 8 (14:29 IST, Oct 13) and Chapter 18 (18:42 IST, Oct 14) — a window that is currently entirely off-page and that runs exactly parallel to Chapters 15–17.

The material is therefore already written, already dated, and already consistent. What is missing is three dramatized scenes in the gap.

## 4. What this buys

- **A live clock in Act III.** The patrol is climbing while Washington argues about custody receipts. The procedural chapters gain something to be measured against.
- **Julie's error stays load-bearing.** Her forty-three seconds put those specific people in an unobserved corridor. Dramatizing the corridor keeps the cost visible instead of retiring it into a finding.
- **A thematic rhyme the book has already written but separated by 40,000 words.** Sharma refusing to let *likely* become *finished*, and Grant refusing to let an absent signature become a finding, are the same act of discipline in two institutions. Intercut, they argue with each other. Sequential, they merely repeat.
- **Ch 18 gets better.** Its strongest material is the cartridge custody chain and the ravine backstory. Removing the retelling concentrates it.

## 5. Constraints

Locked, and any draft violating them is rejected:

1. **No chronology change.** Every existing timestamp, countdown and dateline stands. New scenes fit the measured gaps in §2.
2. **No new proof.** The patrol establishes no fact the K-17 cartridge does not already establish. Rao must not see, infer, or imply an operator, an identity, or an upstream instruction. Existing proof ceilings hold: Tariq's registered authority is not Tariq's presence; the relay's accepted challenge is not a successful commit.
3. **No new named characters.** Rao, Sethi, Pal and Qureshi already exist. Sharma's presence in A only.
4. **The hard stop is preserved exactly.** Rao does not cross the inner boundary, does not pursue the westward tracks, and does not clear the seam. His three stated conditions in Ch 18 are the reasons, dramatized rather than reported.
5. **Pal's medical removal from questioning survives.** Nothing in A–C may let his interrupted words become a complete account.
6. **The ending is untouched.** Chapter 24 and the final line are outside this lock.
7. **No POV expansion.** Rao is the patrol's viewpoint; the book already uses limited third across Julie, Elias, Sarah, Sharma and Grant. Rao is a sixth of the same kind, not a new mode.
